# Viritura

> _Music, flourishing._

## The name

**Viritura** is a coined word, built from two roots:

- **vir-** — from Latin **_viridis_** (green) and **_virēre_** (to be
  green, to flourish, to be alive). The same Proto-Indo-European root
  _\*weyh₁-_ gives us _vital_, _vivid_, and Italian _vita_ (life).
- **-itura** — the Italian/Latin suffix that turns a verb into the
  _act_ or _result_ of doing it. _Scrivere_ → _scrittura_ (writing).
  _Dipingere_ → _pittura_ (painting). _Partire_ → _partitura_ (the
  parted-out score, the orchestral page).

Put together, Viritura is **the act of flourishing** — and, in our
hands, the act of making a score flourish into music.

It is pronounced **vee-ree-TOO-rah**.

## Why this name

The accent color of the app is **deep viridian** (`#215e4e` light /
`#83c8b3` dark tint) — see [Design Language →
Color](?path=/docs/design-language-color--docs). Viridian and _viridis_
share the same root: green not as a hue, but as a sign of _life_,
_newness_, _thriving_.

There is a beautiful precedent for this idea in music history. In the
12th century, the composer, mystic, and polymath **Hildegard von
Bingen** coined the Latin word **_viriditas_** — literally "greenness" —
to mean the divine _life-force_ that flows through living things,
through plants, through bodies, and through art. For Hildegard, music
was _viriditas_ made audible: the greening, animating creative power of
the world.

That is the lineage Viritura claims. We are a tool for **bringing
scores to life** — for taking the static page and giving it the
greening force that turns ink into a living performance.

## What we stand for

In an age where generative AI is encroaching on artistry, Viritura
makes a deliberate counter-bet: **the goal is not to generate music
for humans, but to make it dramatically easier for humans to create
music together.**

The specific gap we close:

- A composer or arranger working in a DAW with sampled instruments
  produces a recording — but **the live musicians who will perform
  that work need parts on a page**.
- Existing notation workflows often treat this as an afterthought, or assume the composer started in
  notation. The pipeline from "MIDI mockup that sounds great" to
  "parts a section player can read at a rehearsal" is broken.
- Viritura is built to **be the bridge** — from arrangement to
  rehearsal, from one composer's idea to a roomful of musicians
  reading from the same page.

The values that follow from that mission:

| Value                 | What it means in practice                                                                                                                                                                           |
| --------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Human-first**       | AI augments the human, never replaces them. We use ML where it removes drudgery (layout, condensing, part extraction) and stay out of the way where artistry lives (voicing, phrasing, expression). |
| **Collaborative**     | A score is rarely written alone. Real-time co-editing, comments tied to musical context, and version history are first-class — not bolted on.                                                       |
| **Performance-ready** | Every feature is judged by the question: _does this help the musicians on the stand?_ Engraving quality, part legibility, and rehearsal workflow are the bar.                                       |
| **Web-native**        | No install, no license dongle, no platform lock-in. Open the URL, open the score.                                                                                                                   |
| **Open by default**   | MNX (the W3C JSON standard) is the native format. Your music is never trapped.                                                                                                                      |

## The identity

Viritura uses the **Folio Display** wordmark: outlined Libertinus Serif
small caps chosen for its connection to score publishing and its
distinctiveness from generic software sans-serifs.

The standalone mark is the **Contained Versal Keyline**: the Folio V
optically centered with clear breathing room inside a deep-viridian
square and contained by a fine warm-paper keyline. It is the favicon,
desktop application icon, and compact brand mark.
